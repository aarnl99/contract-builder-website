How it works

Three steps. No re-typing.

Everything below happens in the browser — nothing to install, nothing to configure.

[ 001 ]

Upload

Drop in a .docx, give it a name, and tag what kind of document it is — NDA, services agreement, offer letter, whatever you draft most.

[ 002 ]

Mark

Select the parts that change from contract to contract — client name, dates, dollar amounts — and label them. The rest of the document, and all its formatting, stays untouched.

[ 003 ]

Draft

Pick the template, fill in a short form, and generate. You get a live preview and a real .docx, ready to send, saved automatically to your library.

Features

Built for the paperwork you redo constantly

Not a generic document editor — a tool for the specific job of turning one contract into many.

Formatting preserved exactly

Bold, italics, and underline are kept run by run, even when a placeholder splits a sentence in the middle. What you mark is what you get back.

Tables and signature blocks included

Signature pages built as tables — the most common layout for them — are fully rendered and markable, not silently dropped.

Real .docx, not a workaround

Every draft comes out as an actual Word file people can open, edit, and send — not a PDF export or a shared link that expires.

A library that sorts itself

Every draft is saved and grouped by document type automatically, and stays traceable back to the master template it came from.

Archive or delete — your call

Hide old drafts without losing them, or remove them permanently. Two distinct actions, because they mean different things.

Email & Slack drafting Coming soon

"Hey Draftly, I need an NDA drafted" — from your inbox or Slack, with the blanks sent back for you to fill in.
